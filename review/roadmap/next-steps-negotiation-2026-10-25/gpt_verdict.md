## Premise attack

| Shared premise, argued against | What the repository establishes; what would decide the rest |
|---|---|
| **“FP3 can be completed in time as a separate owner step.”** A separate early instrument is plausible, but neither candidate identifies an already-approved form that binds D-19’s “C2 drand chain” while the C2 amendment still contains signing and D-19 result markers. Calling a proposed instrument *formal* does not make it operative. A reviewable owner-authored decision, its authority under Constitution §4 and the accepted preregistration, and a committed record binding the chain **before G** would settle this. [Constitution §4](</D:/PMP-programs-for-sharawi/aqt-freeze-sheet/docs/RESEARCH_CONSTITUTION.md>); [preregistration §8](</D:/PMP-programs-for-sharawi/aqt-freeze-sheet/review/governance-statistics-amendment/d19-preregistration/PREREGISTRATION.md>); [unsigned C2 draft §2.0](</D:/PMP-programs-for-sharawi/aqt-freeze-sheet/review/governance-statistics-amendment/s4-amendment-draft/DRAFT_WORDING.md>). |
| **“The laptop can retain the required history through certification.”** The 13,000-block window and mid-January cutoff are projections, not measurements; initial sync and sustained disk capacity are unproved. Conversely, “the same laptop must retain every block through M” is stronger than the cited node setup rule alone: the exact later recheck and acceptable recovery procedure need to be fixed in the reviewed H/I/M sheet. Decide with actual `pruneheight`, complete directory size and free-space readings, the required scan times, and a reviewed M recheck. The current 3,000-block stop rule can force a decision well before any projected cutoff. [Node steps S1–S2](</D:/PMP-programs-for-sharawi/aqt-freeze-sheet/review/fp4-node/FP4_LAPTOP_NODE_STEPS.txt>); [O-6a §4](</D:/PMP-programs-for-sharawi/aqt-freeze-sheet/review/governance-statistics-amendment/o6a-channel/SPEC.md>); [freeze sheet I, K, M](</D:/PMP-programs-for-sharawi/aqt-freeze-sheet/deploy/calibration/FREEZE_HELDOUT.md>). |
| **“January–February is a forecast for step M.”** It is a run-time calculation conditional on FP1 qualifying, a valid FP3 binding, a usable node, the object PR and post, archive capture, drand output, and no substantial 40,000-replication or operational delay. A nonqualifying `choices.json` ends this attempt before the post; there is then no January certification to forecast. The completed development status and choice, measured node window, actual post/freeze records and held-out run plan would turn this into a dated forecast. [Full-run E–F](</D:/PMP-programs-for-sharawi/aqt-freeze-sheet/deploy/calibration/FULL_RUN.md>); [FE-7 assumptions](</D:/PMP-programs-for-sharawi/aqt-freeze-sheet/review/d19-engine/PILOT_FE7_FINDINGS.md>); [freeze sheet G–M](</D:/PMP-programs-for-sharawi/aqt-freeze-sheet/deploy/calibration/FREEZE_HELDOUT.md>). |
| **“The baseline paper clock yields a September 2027 canary floor.”** The arithmetic is a useful *conditional lower bound*, not an established route. The current deployment protocol is a draft, Tasks 31–32 and real orders lack authorization, `L-01` is zero, and whether the baseline can advance without a cycle outcome needs a controlling interpretation. The proposed C2 amendment expressly says only `NO_EDGE_FOUND` opens its baseline path; it is not active law. Actual daily returns and the computed, capped effective count are also needed: service uptime or 240 calendar days cannot substitute for them. A signed applicable deployment protocol, a settled baseline eligibility decision, the paper ledger/ESS report, authorized shadow records and final owner action would decide it. This does not invalidate D-19’s narrower simulated claim. It does mean a future GO must disclose and judge that residual selection risk; a genuinely prospective, declared-before-observation window would supply stronger evidence, at a substantial calendar cost. The strongest shared-premise defect is **baseline eligibility**, because both calendar floors depend on treating a possible route as one the active governance already permits. The repository supports continued paper evidence collection; it does not yet establish a baseline GO route or a canary date.
